# Aesthetic-medicine reporting elements

Use during intake (to know what to ask for) and drafting (to know what must appear).

## 1. Injectables
- Product: generic/material description first, then trade name, manufacturer, country (e.g., "poly-L-lactic acid (Sculptra, Galderma, Sweden)"). Lot number for complication reports.
- Hyaluronic acid: concentration, cross-linking technology if relevant, lidocaine content.
- Dose: volume (mL) or units (U) per site and total; for toxins state the product — units are **not interchangeable** between toxin brands.
- Reconstitution: toxin U per mL and diluent; PLLA diluent volume, lidocaine, hydration time before injection.
- Plane / depth (intradermal, subdermal, subcutaneous, supraperiosteal, intramuscular), needle vs cannula and gauge, technique (bolus, linear retrograde, fanning, microdroplet), entry points (a diagram helps).
- Sessions, interval, touch-ups; post-treatment instructions (e.g., PLLA massage).
- Complication management: hyaluronidase (units, dilution, dose per area, repeat schedule), corticosteroids (drug, concentration, dose), antibiotics (drug, dose, duration), 5-FU, laser, drainage, excision.

## 2. Energy-based devices
Device model and manufacturer; wavelength; fluence (J/cm²) or energy per pulse; pulse width; spot size; repetition rate; passes and overlap; cooling method; clinical endpoint; topical anaesthesia; pre/post care. For RF: tip type, energy level/setting, number of shots.

## 3. Photography and imaging
- Standardise: same camera/system, lighting, distance, background, patient position, neutral expression; views frontal, 45° left/right, 90° left/right; dynamic views for toxin (maximal contraction).
- Imaging systems: name and version (e.g., VISIA, 3D stereophotogrammetry), analysis area identical across time points.
- Ultrasound: probe frequency (MHz), mode (B-mode, Doppler), measurements with calipers shown.
- Figure legends must state timepoint (Day X), view, and any processing (crop only; no retouching).

## 4. Outcome scales
- **GAIS** (Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale), 5-point; say who rated (investigator, independent blinded evaluator, patient) and against which baseline photo.
- Site-specific validated scales where available (glabellar/forehead/crow's-feet lines, mid-face volume, nasolabial folds, lips, tear trough, jawline, neck, skin quality). Name the scale and its validation reference.
- Patient-reported: FACE-Q modules (satisfaction with face/area, appearance-related distress), satisfaction Likert.
- Objective: TEWL, corneometry, cutometry, VISIA indices, 3D volume (mL), ultrasound dermal thickness.
- Blinded photo assessment: randomise photo order and hide timepoint; ≥ 2 independent raters; report agreement (weighted kappa for ordinal, ICC for continuous); identify-the-after-photo accuracy is a useful secondary.

## 5. Adverse events
- Report all events including minor ones (bruising, swelling duration), with onset (day), severity, management, outcome and time to resolution.
- Nodules: early (< 4 weeks) vs late; inflammatory vs non-inflammatory; product vs biofilm vs foreign-body granuloma; imaging findings.
- Vascular events: time to recognition, signs (blanching, livedo, pain, visual symptoms), treatment timeline in minutes/hours.
- Zero events: state the follow-up duration and give the upper 95% bound (≈ 3/n).

## 6. Patient selection and confounders
- Washout from prior treatments (typical minimums used in trials: HA filler 6–12 months, biostimulator 12–24 months, toxin 4–6 months, ablative/non-ablative energy 3–6 months — state what was actually required).
- Stable weight (GLP-1 RA use is now a common confounder), no concurrent treatments or new active skincare, season/sun exposure for pigmentation outcomes, smoking.
- Body dysmorphic disorder: note screening (e.g., BDDQ) or that it was not performed.
- For split-face: baseline symmetry, allocation method, risk of cross-over effect (toxin diffusion, systemic effects) — split-face suits locally acting treatments.

## 7. Consent and ethics
- Written consent for publication, explicitly covering identifiable facial images. Eye bars do not anonymise.
- Off-label use: consent for the treatment itself; ethics committee approval or documented exemption.
- Retrospective series: ethics approval or waiver per local rules.
- Industry: free product, honoraria, trainer roles → declare.

## 8. Language and product naming
- Neutral, non-promotional: "improvement", not "dramatic transformation"; no superlatives; no brand in the title unless the journal allows it.
- Report what was observed; no efficacy claims beyond the design (a case report cannot show superiority).
- Use "patient", not "client"/"customer"; "treatment", not "procedure package".
